Smooth, clipped, or switched.
The neural Jacobian applies the chain rule through three tanh layers and a sigmoid. It is a first-order approximation, not an exact formula for a finite edit. PCA coefficients solve a fixed observed-only ridge system. Their response is affine before output clipping; the local derivative changes when an output reaches a clipping boundary.
Nearest-neighbor retrieval is different. The selected row can switch across a distance boundary. The third image freezes the original neighbor's hidden pixels, rather than pretending a useful smooth derivative exists across that switch. With fitting rows [0,0] and [1,1], observing only the first pixel at 0.5 chooses the lower index. In ideal arithmetic, increasing it by any positive amount chooses the other row, jumping the hidden prediction from 0 to 1.
This is one fixed model, historical public block-count digits and artificial masks. Not natural-missingness validation, uncertainty estimation, image generation or reliable restoration.
